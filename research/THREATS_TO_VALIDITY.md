# Threats to validity

This file is about the method-discovery gate, not about a new experiment. No new pairs were scored.

## Construct

The question was whether a decision procedure for this `+` `-` `*` fragment can be both new and sound. "New" was judged against sources that were actually retrieved. A paper that was not opened cannot be used to invent a distinction, and it also cannot be used to invent a citation.

The internal review is not an independent review. A second researcher might still find a narrow algorithm this gate missed. That possibility does not justify implementing a rejected candidate.

## Internal

The polynomial oracle, the exhaustive oracle, and the SMT encodings share parser and integer-semantics code. Historical agreement among them does not prove the translator. This gate does not repeat that agreement as a new proof.

Mode A and Mode B were not mixed in the candidate statements. A later implementation that mixed them would be invalid even if this gate were reopened.

## External

Results on this fragment do not transfer to LLVM, to Alive2, or to languages with division, shifts, or memory. Alive2 was not run.

The 463-pair census and the 72-pair confirmatory set describe the existing solvers. They are not evidence about an unimplemented method. They were not rerun.

## Statistical

No interval was computed for H_new. An empty denominator is not a zero rate. The analysis script is required to print `pair_count` 0 and `false_equivalence` null.

Historical intervals, including Wilson bounds on 0/96 and 0/33, stay attached to those old denominators.

## Conclusion validity

The NO-GO conclusion is only that no candidate met the novelty and soundness gate in this review. It is not a proof that no future algorithm exists. It is a decision not to claim one.
