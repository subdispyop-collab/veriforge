## Text used

All submissions are online only - no email or physical submissions accepted. The synopsis is structured in six sections with the word limits below, plus a short project video:

01 Abstract 250 words. A concise summary of your whole project — the problem, your approach, key results and conclusion. Written last, but the first thing judges read. The submission portal enforces a 300-word hard cap.

02 Introduction & Objective 100–150 words. The background and context of your problem, why it matters, and the specific aim or hypothesis your project sets out to address.

03 Innovation 50–100 words. What makes your project original — the novel idea, method or improvement that sets it apart from existing solutions.

04 Methodology 150–250 words. Your step-by-step process — materials, experimental design, procedures, and how you collected and analysed your data so others could repeat it.

05 Results & Conclusions 100–150 words. Your key findings and what they mean — whether your hypothesis held, backed by your data, and the wider implications of the work.

06 Acknowledgement & References 50–100 words. Credit to the mentors and institutions who helped you, plus a properly cited list of every source and prior study you referenced.

07 Project Video Max 90 sec. A short video (90 seconds maximum) where you present and explain your project clearly to the judges. Hosted on YouTube (unlisted or public) — the portal takes the URL, not a file upload.

You should also maintain a research data book with detailed notes, alongside the synopsis and a full research paper (with title page, table of contents, introduction, methodology, discussion, conclusion, acknowledgements and references). The research paper has no strict word limit — aim for roughly 10–15 pages. A single guide per project is required.

Synopsis PDF ≤ 5 MB. Research paper PDF ≤ 20 MB. Project video YouTube URL ≤ 90 sec. The video is submitted as a YouTube link (unlisted or public) — the portal does not accept MP4 uploads. The abstract has a 300-word hard cap enforced by the portal; drafts above that limit will be rejected.

Open to students of Indian origin enrolled at India-based schools. Projects submitted individually or as a team of two - original, research-based or a novel engineering design.

- Indian origin, Classes 5–12. Students residing and studying in schools based in India - government, semi-government and private institutions all welcome.
- Individual or team of two. Projects may be submitted individually or as a team of exactly two students. Teams of three or more are not accepted.
- Original & innovative thinking. Work must demonstrate original research with experimental data, or novel engineering improvements over existing solutions.
- Recent data only. Experimental data cannot exceed 12 months old, though projects may extend previously started work.

Projects must be submitted under one of 22 ISEF-aligned subject categories. The Scientific Review Committee may reclassify projects as needed based on volume.

Rule 01 · Anonymity. No projects are allowed to disclose their school’s name, city name or state in their project video, research paper, project report, presentation, or abstract. Anonymity is enforced throughout evaluation.

Rule 02 · Data Freshness. Experimental data cannot exceed 12 months old at the time of submission. You may extend previously started work, but recent data must fall within this window.
